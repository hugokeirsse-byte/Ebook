How to Use This Book

A complete guide to getting the most from your 90-day tracker

This journal is structured to give you and your veterinarian the most useful, detailed picture of your dog's epilepsy over a 90-day period. The information you record here can directly influence treatment decisions, medication adjustments, and quality-of-life improvements for your dog.

1

Complete the Setup Pages First

Before Day 1, fill in the Emergency Information page, Dog Profile, Veterinary Contacts, and Current Medications List. Keep this book accessible to anyone who might care for your dog.

2

Use the Reference Pages

Familiarize yourself with the Seizure Type Reference, Post-Ictal Behavior Guide, Common Triggers List, and Seizure Severity Scale before logging. These will help you record accurately and consistently.

3

Fill in Each Daily Log Page

Complete the daily log every evening, or immediately following any seizure event. Record medications given that day whether or not a seizure occurred — consistent daily data is the most valuable data.

4

Record Every Seizure in Detail

If a seizure occurs, complete the full Seizure Event section: time, duration, type, severity, pre-ictal signs, during-seizure behavior, and post-ictal observations. Each detail matters clinically.

5

Complete Monthly Summaries

At the end of each 30-day period, fill in the Monthly Summary page. This provides a quick visual overview of seizure frequency and patterns your vet can review at a glance.

6

Bring This Book to Every Vet Appointment

Your records here replace the guesswork. Veterinary neurologists and general practitioners will be able to make faster, better-informed decisions when they can review 90 days of documented data.

7

Use the Trigger Analysis Worksheet

After each month, review the Trigger Analysis Worksheet to identify patterns: time of day, activities, foods, stress events, or weather changes that correlate with seizure occurrence.

How to Use This Book

Understanding what to record and when

What to Record When No Seizure Occurs

✓Morning and evening medication doses (given / not given / vomited)
✓Overall behavior and energy level
✓Appetite and water intake (especially relevant for phenobarbital and KBr)
✓Any unusual behavior, ataxia, sedation, or side effects
✓Exercise, stress, changes in routine
✓Sleep quality

What to Record During / After a Seizure

✓Start time and end time — use a clock or phone timer immediately
✓Seizure type — generalized (grand mal), focal, focal with secondary generalization
✓Pre-ictal signs (aura) — restlessness, hiding, attention-seeking, salivation before onset
✓During seizure — limb paddling, loss of consciousness, jaw chomping, urination, defecation
✓Post-ictal duration — confusion, blindness, aggression, hunger, exhaustion after
✓Cluster — note if more than one seizure occurred within 24 hours
✓Emergency medication — if rectal diazepam or other rescue was administered

When to Call Your Vet Immediately

⚠Seizure lasting more than 5 minutes (status epilepticus — veterinary emergency)
⚠More than 2 seizures within 24 hours (cluster seizures)
⚠Dog does not recover to normal within 30–60 minutes post-seizure
⚠First seizure ever observed in your dog
⚠Seizure following head trauma, toxin exposure, or known ingestion
