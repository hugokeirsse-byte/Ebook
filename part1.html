How to Use This Book

A complete guide to getting the most from your 90-day tracker

This journal gives you and your veterinarian the most detailed picture of your dog's epilepsy over a 90-day period. The data you record can directly influence treatment decisions and medication adjustments.

1

Complete the Setup Pages First

Before Day 1, fill in Emergency Information, Dog Profile, Veterinary Contacts, and Current Medications. Keep this book accessible to anyone who cares for your dog.

2

Read the Reference Pages

Review the Seizure Type Reference, Post-Ictal Guide, Common Triggers, and Severity Scale before you begin logging. Consistent use of these codes makes your data clinically meaningful.

3

Fill In Each Daily Log Every Evening

Complete the daily log whether or not a seizure occurred. Medication compliance data on seizure-free days is just as important as seizure data.

4

Record Every Seizure in Full Detail

Capture start and end time, seizure type, severity, pre-ictal signs, during-seizure behavior, and post-ictal observations. Use a phone timer the moment a seizure begins.

5

Complete Monthly Summaries

At the end of each 30-day period, fill in the Monthly Summary page for a quick visual overview your vet can review at a glance.

6

Bring This Book to Every Vet Appointment

90 days of documented data replaces guesswork. Your veterinarian and neurologist will make faster, better-informed decisions with this record in hand.

7

Use the Trigger Analysis Worksheet

After each month, review the Trigger Worksheet to identify patterns — time of day, activities, foods, stress events, or weather changes correlated with seizures.

How to Use This Book

What to record and when

What to Record When No Seizure Occurs

Morning and evening medication doses (given / late / missed / vomited)
Overall behavior and energy level
Appetite and water intake — especially relevant for phenobarbital and KBr
Any ataxia, sedation, or medication side effects observed
Exercise level, stress events, or changes in routine
Sleep quality

What to Record During / After a Seizure

Start and end time — use a phone timer immediately
Seizure type — GTC, Focal, FSG, Cluster, or SE (see Reference pages)
Pre-ictal signs — restlessness, hiding, salivation, attention-seeking before onset
During seizure — paddling, LOC, jaw chomping, urination, defecation
Post-ictal duration — confusion, blindness, ataxia, hunger, exhaustion after
Rescue medication — if diazepam or other rescue drug was administered

⚠ When to Call Your Vet Immediately

Seizure lasting more than 5 minutes — status epilepticus, veterinary emergency
More than 2 seizures within 24 hours — cluster seizures
Dog does not recover to baseline within 30–60 minutes post-seizure
First seizure ever observed in your dog
Seizure following trauma, toxin exposure, or suspected ingestion
