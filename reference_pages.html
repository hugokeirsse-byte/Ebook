Seizure Type Quick Reference

Use these descriptions when filling in your daily log

Generalized Seizures (Grand Mal / Tonic-Clonic)

→Both brain hemispheres affected simultaneously
→Tonic phase: sudden muscle stiffness, dog falls to side, loss of consciousness
→Clonic phase: rhythmic paddling or jerking of all four limbs
→May include jaw chomping, excessive salivation, urination, defecation
→Dog is unconscious and unresponsive during event
→Typical duration: 30 seconds – 3 minutes

Log Code: GTC

Focal Seizures (Partial)

→Limited to one area of the brain — may affect one limb, one side of the face, or specific behaviors
→Motor signs: twitching of a single limb, facial twitching, repetitive blinking, head turning
→Autonomic signs: dilated pupils, vomiting, salivation without full seizure
→Behavioral (limbic): unprovoked fear, aggression, fly-biting, staring episodes, rhythmic licking
→Dog may remain conscious and aware

Log Code: FOC

Focal with Secondary Generalization

→Begins as focal seizure, then spreads to full generalized tonic-clonic event
→Watch for brief focal signs (twitching, head turn) immediately before the generalized phase begins
→Often the pre-ictal phase IS the focal component

Log Code: FSG

Cluster Seizures

⚠Two or more seizures within a 24-hour period — requires veterinary contact
⚠Each seizure may be of any type listed above
⚠Risk of hyperthermia and neurological damage increases with each successive event

Log Code: CLU

Status Epilepticus

⚠⚠Single seizure lasting 5+ minutes OR series of seizures without full recovery between — VETERINARY EMERGENCY
→Call emergency clinic immediately. Do not wait for the seizure to stop on its own.
→Administer rescue medication if prescribed and instructed to do so

Log Code: SE

Post-Ictal Behavior Reference

What happens after the seizure — and what to record

The post-ictal period begins when the active seizure ends and lasts until the dog returns to its normal baseline. Duration varies from minutes to several hours. Recording this phase is clinically important — it can indicate seizure severity and brain recovery.

Post-Ictal Sign What It Looks Like Log Code
Disorientation / Confusion Blank staring, bumping into walls, appearing lost in familiar spaces CONF
Temporary Blindness Does not track movement, bumps into objects, wide pupils unresponsive to light BLIND
Ataxia Wobbly gait, stumbling, difficulty standing, falling ATAX
Extreme Fatigue / Sleep Immediate and prolonged deep sleep following the event SLEP
Hyperphagia Intense, voracious hunger immediately post-ictal — common with phenobarbital users HUNG
Polydipsia Excessive, compulsive drinking DRNK
Agitation / Pacing Restless, repetitive walking circuits, unable to settle AGIT
Aggression Uncharacteristic growling, snapping, biting — approach carefully, dog does not recognize owner AGGR
Vocalization Whining, howling, barking without apparent cause VOCA
Normal Recovery Dog returns to baseline quickly with minimal signs — positive indicator NORM

How to Provide Safe Support During Post-Ictal Phase

✓Stay calm and quiet — avoid loud voices or sudden movements near the dog
✓Do not restrain the dog unless it is in immediate danger of injuring itself
✓Dim the lights and reduce stimulation — light and sound sensitivity are common
✓Offer water when the dog appears oriented enough to drink safely
✓Do not place your face near the dog's mouth during agitation or aggression post-ictally
✓Record the time recovery to baseline begins and when it completes

Common Seizure Triggers

Use these categories when recording possible triggers in your daily log

Note: Not all seizures have an identifiable trigger. Recording potential triggers consistently over 90 days allows pattern analysis. Mark suspected triggers with a "?" if you're unsure.

Physiological

•Missed medication dose
•Late medication dose (timing shift >1hr)
•Illness / fever
•Pain
•Fasting / low blood sugar
•Dehydration
•Heat / overheating
•Hormonal cycle (intact females)

Environmental

•Flashing / flickering lights
•Loud noises (fireworks, thunder)
•Extreme weather change
•Strong chemical odors
•Travel / new environment
•Extreme heat or cold
•Barometric pressure change

Behavioral / Emotional

•High excitement / arousal
•Anxiety / fear
•Separation anxiety event
•Visitors / new people
•Conflict with other pets
•Routine disruption
•Owner absence / long time alone

Activity / Physical

•Intense exercise
•Playing fetch / high-arousal play
•Swimming
•Grooming / bathing
•Waking from deep sleep
•Overstimulation at dog park
•Sleep deprivation

Dietary

•Food change / new diet
•Treats with artificial additives
•Rawhide / specific chew treats
•Large meal before exercise
•Fasting (skipped meal)
•Xylitol / toxic food exposure

Medical / Chemical

•Vaccination (within 48 hrs)
•Anesthesia / sedation
•New supplement started
•Flea/tick product applied
•Lawn chemicals / pesticides
•Household cleaning products

When in doubt, record it. Patterns are only visible when data is consistent. A trigger that appears once may be coincidence; the same trigger appearing before 60% of seizures is clinically significant.

Seizure Severity Scale

Use this scale consistently in your daily log for meaningful trend analysis

This scale is adapted for canine epilepsy home monitoring. Circle or write the number in your daily log. Consistent use of the same scale allows your veterinarian to track progression or improvement over time.

1

MILD — Brief Focal or Absent-Like Event

Duration under 30 seconds. Focal only (e.g. single limb twitch, brief stare, fly-biting). Dog remains conscious. No loss of balance. Rapid return to normal (<5 minutes). No intervention required.

2

MILD-MODERATE — Short Generalized Event

Duration 30 seconds – 1 minute. Full generalized tonic-clonic with loss of consciousness. Mild salivation. Brief post-ictal period (5–15 minutes). Dog recovers fully. No emergency medication needed.

3

MODERATE — Standard Generalized Event

Duration 1–3 minutes. Full generalized seizure with pronounced signs (urination, defecation, severe paddling). Post-ictal period 15–60 minutes with notable disorientation, ataxia, or temporary blindness. Dog recovers fully. No emergency medication administered.

4

SEVERE — Prolonged or Cluster Event

Duration 3–5 minutes, OR more than one seizure within 24 hours. Emergency medication administered. Post-ictal period >1 hour with pronounced signs. Veterinary contact made. Dog recovered at home without ER visit.

5

CRITICAL — Status Epilepticus / Emergency

Seizure exceeds 5 minutes OR seizures occur in series without full recovery between events. Emergency medication administered. Veterinary emergency visit required. Dog required hospitalization or IV anticonvulsant therapy.
